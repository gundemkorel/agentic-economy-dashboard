# Research Definitions

These definitions are fixed working terms for the dashboard. Update them only through a documented methodology change; do not redefine a term to fit a preferred stock conclusion.

## Wave 1 — Intelligence

Models generate content, analysis, or reasoning. The primary economic activity is model training and inference.

## Wave 2 — Activity

Models interact with digital systems and take, coordinate, or prepare actions. Relevant events may include retrieval, API calls, context lookup, authentication, policy checks, telemetry, retries, verification, communications, and tool use. Wave 2 does not require full financial autonomy.

## Wave 3 — Economy

Agents receive delegated authority involving money, procurement, commerce, logistics, or physical execution. It remains a separate observation / discovery track and is not mixed into the current Wave-2 signal.

## Machine activity

Observable nonhuman activity generated by automation, bots, models, tools, or agents. It is broader than productive agentic activity.

## AI bot

A source-defined category of automated AI-related traffic. Depending on the source, it can include training crawlers, search crawlers, fetchers, malicious traffic, scraping, or user-directed requests. It is never treated as a direct count of productive agents.

## AI crawler

Automated traffic that discovers or indexes content, often for model training or search. It may create infrastructure cost but does not by itself establish an action-oriented agentic workflow.

## AI fetcher / action-oriented traffic

A source-defined request class associated with targeted retrieval, tool use, or work performed on a user or agent’s behalf. It is a more relevant proxy for Wave 2 than crawler volume, but it remains incomplete and source-specific.

## Autonomous agent

Software that can plan, select tools, interact with systems, and execute bounded tasks with reduced human intervention. A chat interface that generates an answer is not automatically an autonomous agent.

## AI-mediated enterprise activity

Existing enterprise information flow increasingly passed through models for classification, extraction, retrieval, routing, policy, or action. It is a potential second multiplier and should not be inferred merely from model adoption.

## Agentic revenue elasticity

The degree to which a company’s revenue grows mechanically when agentic activity rises, holding the rest of the business constant. It requires a named billable unit and pricing architecture; product relevance is insufficient.

## Action intensity

A qualitative research concept describing the mix of action-, fetcher-, tool-, or user-directed activity relative to passive crawler activity. The dashboard does not calculate a synthetic index until robust comparable source data exists.

## Company capture

Evidence that a named company converts relevant activity into durable revenue, retention, margins, or cash flow. Macro demand is not company capture.

## Expectation gap

A possible divergence between improving, causal operating evidence and what is reflected in fiscal-period-aligned analyst estimates and valuation. A current quote, price target, product launch, or single consensus snapshot cannot establish a gap. The dashboard requires aligned conventions and at least two point-in-time consensus and valuation observations before making a gap call.

## Scenario assumption

The project’s own stated input used to examine outcomes under alternative paths. It is visibly separate from observed data, company reporting, and analyst consensus. A scenario is not a forecast or investment recommendation.
