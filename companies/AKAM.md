# AKAM — Akamai

## Dated gap review — September 24, 2026

**Research state: monitor / early capture.** Akamai has credible evidence that AI-adjacent infrastructure and control-plane demand is reaching its business. It has not yet disclosed enough agent-specific revenue, conversion, pricing, or margin evidence to support a quantified expectation-gap claim.

This is a research conclusion, not an investment recommendation.

## Wave-2 mechanism

More autonomous web activity could increase the need for edge compute, traffic delivery, API and bot controls, identity checks, and security decisions. Akamai benefits only if it retains that workload and charges incrementally for the capacity or control plane.

## What is observed

- **Cloud infrastructure is growing:** Q2 2026 Cloud Infrastructure Services (CIS) revenue was $99.3 million, up 39% year over year. The category was about 9% of Q2 revenue; it is growing quickly but is not yet the whole business.
- **Security remains relevant:** Security revenue was $604.4 million, up 10% year over year.
- **Orders are material:** Akamai disclosed more than $2.8 billion of CIS commitments year to date, including a greater-than-$600 million, four-year contract for robotics development.
- **The company is preparing to identify agent traffic:** In September, its Bot Directory separated AI training crawlers, AI search crawlers, and AI fetchers and agents. This is useful product and measurement readiness, not evidence of customer adoption or price realization.

## Why the capture case is not proven yet

- Akamai does not separately report agentic-AI revenue, Bot & Agent Control ARR, attach rates, pricing, or gross margin.
- Delivery and other cloud applications revenue fell 6% year over year in Q2, driven in part by renewal pricing pressure and customer cost optimization.
- The Q2 non-GAAP operating margin was 25%, down five percentage points year over year.
- The 10-Q says that server, memory, co-location, bandwidth, depreciation, and other costs will rise as Akamai expands CIS and AI capacity. The crucial question is revenue quality after that investment, not merely capacity growth.
- Network bot statistics include unwanted bots, scraping, and security events. They must not be treated as productive agentic consumption.

## Expectation-gap discipline

The live [Gap Readiness dashboard](https://gundemkorel.github.io/agentic-economy-dashboard/expectations.html) carries an attributed, current consensus snapshot. It should be compared with company guidance only after fiscal-year and definition alignment, and it cannot establish a gap without revision and valuation history. Do not store or rely on a stale consensus number in this research note.

The current decision rule is simple: **do not call an upside gap merely because CIS is growing or agent-control products exist.** The case improves only if reported conversion and profitability develop faster than the live consensus view can absorb.

## What would upgrade the thesis

1. CIS growth remains strong across subsequent quarters and the disclosed commitments convert into recognized revenue on a visible schedule.
2. Management quantifies AI / inference / agent-control revenue, customer adoption, price realization, or cross-sell economics.
3. Security growth includes credible agent-control attach evidence rather than only product announcements.
4. Delivery declines stabilize while the CIS build-out reaches a clearer margin and free-cash-flow trajectory.

## Thesis breakers

1. Large CIS commitments convert more slowly than expected, or prove too capital-intensive to create attractive incremental returns.
2. Agentic traffic is bundled into existing security contracts with little incremental price or retention benefit.
3. Delivery pricing pressure and traffic optimization outweigh growth in CIS and security.
4. The observed rise in bots is primarily hostile, scraping, or zero-priced traffic rather than billable autonomous activity.

## Quarterly checklist

- CIS revenue, growth, bookings / commitment conversion, and customer concentration.
- Security growth, Bot & Agent Control disclosure, and any agent-specific ARR or pricing metrics.
- Delivery revenue, renewal pricing, traffic trends, and customer optimization commentary.
- Capital expenditure, co-location, server / memory costs, gross margin, operating margin, and free cash flow.
- Current, attributed consensus versus management outlook only after period alignment.
